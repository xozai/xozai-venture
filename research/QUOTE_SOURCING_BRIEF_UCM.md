# Quote sourcing brief — UCM

## Why this exists

`research/OPPORTUNITY_SCAN_2026-08-30.md` records the same gap twice: for UCM,
"**Verbatim pain quotes:** none surfaced," after "multiple targeted searches
(Eng-Tips, r/civilengineering, YouTube-comment-style queries)."

The `go-to-market` skill gates my content work on exactly this (gate item 3:
"Verbatim pain quotes with sources, from `research/`"), and `pitch-deck`
inherits the same constraint through Anti-BS Rule 11. So this is not a nice-to-
have. Until a real quote exists, the landing headline has to be written by us
about the buyer instead of in the buyer's words, which is the failure mode my
rubric weights heaviest (`research/MARKETABILITY_RUBRIC.md`, named pain ×3).

## The hypothesis: we searched the wrong corpus, not a corpus that's empty

The prior searches assume practitioner complaints live on public forums. For
this specific population, that assumption is probably wrong — and it is wrong
in a way that has a fix.

Per the scan itself, the UCM buyer is "an individual PE/EIT at a private
transportation or land-development design firm," and the methodology is
"FHWA's SHRP2 UCM methodology," used by and with state DOTs. That is a
population whose professional discourse is *captured and published as public
record* — surveys, committee minutes, audits, legislative testimony, trade
press — rather than posted pseudonymously. Utility coordination is also
adversarial and claim-adjacent (delay, relocation cost, redesign), which is a
strong disincentive to complain in public under a real name on a forum, and no
disincentive at all to say the same thing on the record in a survey response or
an oversight hearing.

This is the same structural move that unlocked quotes on the Strata track:
council minutes and meeting video are public record, so attributable verbatim
complaints are free once you look at the record instead of at social media.

## Where to look, ranked by expected verbatim density

Each of these is a **hypothesis to verify**, not a claim that the document
exists or contains a usable quote. Ordered by how likely the format is to
print a practitioner's own words rather than an institutional summary.

1. **NCHRP / TRB synthesis reports on utility coordination, utility
   relocation, and SUE.** Synthesis reports are built from practitioner
   surveys and interviews, and the genre convention is to print open-ended
   responses verbatim, usually in an appendix. Highest expected density of
   "an engineer's own words" of anything in this list. Free full text via
   nap.edu; search TRID.
2. **FHWA SHRP2 R15B implementation / pilot-assistance reporting.** The scan
   already credits FHWA with the UCM methodology and with the "stand-alone,
   spreadsheet-based matrix (UCM lite)" description. Implementation Assistance
   Program write-ups typically include participating-agency feedback on what
   the tool did and did not do in practice.
3. **State auditor / inspector-general and legislative-oversight reports on
   utility relocation delay.** Audits interview and quote agency staff, are
   public record, and are specifically about the *cost of this going wrong* —
   which is the before-state our copy needs.
4. **AASHTO Committee on Right of Way, Utilities and Outdoor Advertising**
   meeting materials and state DOT utility-coordination training materials.
   Committee minutes and training Q&A capture practitioner framing directly.
5. **Trade press practitioner columns** — APWA *Reporter*, IRWA *Right of Way*
   magazine. Bylined practitioners describing their own workflow; usable and
   attributable, though closer to advocacy than complaint.
6. **Conference presentation slides and recorded sessions** (UESI, APWA, state
   DOT utility conferences). Speaker notes and Q&A often contain the blunt
   version that the paper does not.

If 1–3 come back empty after a genuine pass, that is itself a finding worth
recording — it moves "unevidenced" meaningfully closer to "the complaint isn't
there," which changes the UCM decision, not just the copy.

## What a usable quote looks like

I need quotes I can put in a headline or an ad. Spec:

**Usable:**
- First person, present tense, about the *task*: what they do, how long it
  takes, how often it breaks, what they re-key from where.
- Names the artifact or the workaround (the spreadsheet, the matrix, the
  version that's out of date, the utility that never replied).
- Attributable: a name and role, or at minimum a stated role and agency type
  plus a citable document and page.

**Not usable — do not send these:**
- Institutional framing of consequences ("utility conflicts are a leading
  cause of project delay"). The scan already flags exactly this failure mode:
  it "confirms the *cost* of getting this wrong ... but that's institutional
  framing, not an engineer's own words." It cannot become a headline.
- Vendor or consultant marketing copy describing the problem their service
  solves.
- Paraphrase, or a quote without a locatable source.

**Volume:** 8–12 candidates with URLs and page/timestamp references. I expect
to discard most; the discard rate is why I want a dozen, not three.

## What this brief does not do

It does not rank UCM, re-open the TIS-vs-UCM decision, or authorize content.
Per the scan, the ranking is already robust to this gap. What is *not* robust
to it is the copy — and that is the part I own.
