# Why this exists

`research/MARKETABILITY_RUBRIC.md` was calibrated against civil engineering: a solo PE
who buys on a card, a licensure system that hands us near-free webinar distribution, and
small private firms that will go on record next month. Enterprise SPM has none of those
properties. Applying the civil anchors literally to an SPM table produces scores that are
wrong in a specific, predictable direction, so this file re-anchors the dimensions.

**Read this before scoring the SPM table. Do not score SPM with the civil anchors.**

## The artifact this prevents

Three dimensions are structurally different in enterprise, independent of any candidate's
merit. Scored on civil anchors, every SPM candidate takes the same hit:

| Dimension | Civil anchor value | SPM reality on civil anchors | Raw cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer = user (×3) | 5 — individual PE, card, discretionary | 2 — RevOps + CRO + often CFO/IT | −9 |
| Channel fit (×2) | 5 — PDH-credit webinars, PE societies | 3 — real communities, but no free-distribution equivalent | −4 |
| Reference velocity (×1) | 4 — small private firm says yes | 2 — enterprise logo needs legal and comms sign-off | −2 |

Take the civil #2 candidate (UCM, 80) and imagine an SPM candidate of **identical merit**
in its own category. Scored on civil anchors it lands at **61**. That is a 19-point,
two-band gap produced entirely by the scale, not by the candidate.

This is the same failure mode the base rubric already warns about with market size: a
property of the measuring instrument getting read as a finding about the thing measured.

## Two rules

1. **Re-anchor per space.** Use the anchors below for SPM so that within the SPM table,
   scores discriminate between candidates instead of all compressing at the bottom.
2. **Never compare marketability scores across tracks.** Re-anchoring fixes ranking
   *within* a track; it does not make a civil 80 and an SPM 80 mean the same thing, because
   5 denotes "best achievable in this space" and the spaces differ. When joseleos picks
   across tracks, he gets a structural comparison in prose, not two numbers side by side.
   A cross-track numeric ranking is the one output this rubric must not produce.

## SPM anchors

**1. Named pain (×3).** Unchanged in meaning, and SPM is *easier* to evidence than civil —
this is the one dimension where the new track has the advantage. G2/Capterra 1–3★ reviews
of Xactly, Varicent, SAP Commissions and Anaplan are unprompted, attributed, indexed
complaint, tied to a named incumbent. r/salesops and comp-analyst LinkedIn posts add more.
The forum blind spot that blocked the civil scan does not apply here.
*5* = multiple 1–3★ incumbent reviews plus community threads naming this specific task.
*1* = we infer the pain from a workflow description.

**2. Time-to-obvious (×3).** Unchanged. SPM does well structurally — a modeled comp plan or
a generated commission statement is legible in one screenshot.
*5* = before/after fits in one frame. *1* = value is a percentage-point lift provable only
after a full comp cycle.

**3. Buying-committee friction (×3)** — replaces "buyer = user," which degenerates in
enterprise where the buyer is essentially never the user.
*5* = a single comp analyst or RevOps owner can trial it and expense it under their
discretionary limit; land-and-expand is available.
*3* = RevOps leader plus CRO sign-off.
*1* = CFO, IT security review, SOC 2, procurement, and a payroll-system integration
approval before a dollar moves.
This version discriminates properly: a plan-modeling tool an analyst trials on Tuesday is a
genuinely different sale from a system of record that touches payroll.

**4. Channel fit (×2).** Re-anchored downward — the ceiling here is honestly lower than
civil's, and pretending otherwise inflates the whole track. There is no PDH equivalent;
licensure-driven free distribution does not exist in this market.
*5* = active standing in RevOps Co-op / Pavilion, a G2 category presence, and a working
LinkedIn creator motion (the audience genuinely lives there, and it is cheap if we have a
voice).
*3* = one of those, plus paid.
*1* = conference-and-outbound only, competing on spend against thirteen funded incumbents.

**5. Liability drag, inverse (×2).** Use the analog from the space profile: does the output
determine money paid to employees or booked in audited financials?
*5* = advisory or analytical; nobody's paycheck moves.
*1* = it produces the number on the paycheck, or the ASC 606 accrual.
Sharper than civil's stamp problem in one respect: a wrong commission is felt personally
and immediately by every rep, and is legally actionable as a wage claim. The implication
for candidate selection is directional — the low-drag ground in SPM is the design,
modeling, diagnostic and communication layer, not the calculation engine.

**6. Wedge narrative (×2).** "It replaces the spreadsheet" is genuinely available here —
a large share of comp still runs in Excel — so a 5 is reachable.
*5* = replaces a self-built spreadsheet or a manual analyst process, no vendor named.
*1* = "a better Xactly."
Caution: the *core* calc-engine ground is contested by well-funded mid-market players
(CaptivateIQ, Spiff/Salesforce, Everstage). The uncontested spreadsheet ground sits at the
edges — plan design, dispute handling, comp communication — not in the middle.

**7. Reference velocity (×1).** Re-anchored down across the board.
*5* = design partners willing to be named within two quarters.
*1* = logo requires legal and comms approval that will not arrive this year.

## One SPM-only capture, feeding dimension 6

**AI credibility burden.** In civil, "AI-native" was itself differentiating — the prior
scan found no AI-native competitor in the OPCC niche. In SPM, effectively all thirteen
named incumbents already market AI. So "AI-powered" is not a claim here, it is noise, and
positioning that leans on it drowns.

Capture per candidate: **can we state in one concrete sentence what the AI does that an
incumbent cannot say?** If the honest answer is no, the candidate cannot score above 3 on
wedge narrative regardless of how good the underlying product is, because we would be
entering a category where our central claim is already ambient.

This is a headwind specific to this track. It is not in the base rubric because civil never
needed it.
