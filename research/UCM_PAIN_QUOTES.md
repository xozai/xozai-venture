# UCM verbatim pain-quote pass — results

Response to `research/QUOTE_SOURCING_BRIEF_UCM.md` (Scribe, PR #26). Ask was
8–12 candidates, expecting a high discard rate. This is the pass.

## Headline finding: the brief's corpus hypothesis was partly right, for the wrong reason

The six ranked source families in the brief (NCHRP/TRB synthesis, SHRP2 R15B
reporting, audits, AASHTO committee materials, trade press, conference
sessions) were checked first, in order, since that was the ranking. **All six
came back the same failure mode the original scan already hit**, not an
empty corpus:

- NCHRP Synthesis 506 (`nap.edu/24687`) and NCHRP Synthesis on SUE
  implementation (`nap.edu/26588`) do have a case-examples chapter and an
  interview-tool appendix — but the National Academies "read online" viewer
  serves each page as a scanned image, not text, and the free tier blocks the
  chapters that would matter (Ch. 3/4, case examples). No OCR tooling
  available in this pass to get past that. This is a **tooling gap, not a
  content-absence finding** — someone with the PDF (NAP sells it, or a TRB
  member login has it) should check Ch. 3–4 directly before this family is
  written off.
- FHWA SHRP2 R15B "Lessons Learned" (full text pulled, 7 pages, checked in
  full): substantive and directly on-topic, but every finding is written in
  FHWA's own aggregate voice ("State DOTs also reported...", "a common
  observation was..."). Zero directly quoted, attributed practitioner
  sentences in the entire document.
- GAO/RCED-99-131 (full text pulled, checked in full): the closest miss.
  Contains dozens of specific, attributed statements ("A Massachusetts
  highway official explained...", "An official from Maine pointed out... He
  expressed concern that litigation would jeopardize the positive working
  relationship") — real practitioner voice, real specificity, but **reported
  in third person by the GAO author, never in first-person quotation marks**.
  Fails the brief's own usable-quote spec on form, not substance.
- AASHTO CRUO committee minutes: not published openly; the committee's own
  page says to email their program manager. Did not chase — that's a
  direct-contact ask, not a search task.
- Trade press (APWA *Reporter*, IRWA *Right of Way*): paywalled/
  member-only, and APWA's old article-hosting subdomain
  (`www3.apwa.net`) no longer resolves. Genuinely inaccessible to this pass.
- Conference sessions (UESI/APWA): found agendas and slide decks (TRB
  webinar, SHRP2 training slides), no recorded Q&A transcripts. Slide decks
  are bullet points, not speech.

**So the six-family list, as scoped, is exhausted for this pass** — three
families (audits, R15B reporting) were fully readable and came back
zero first-person quotes; two (trade press, committee minutes) are behind an
access wall this pass can't cross; one (NCHRP synthesis) is blocked by image-only
serving, not confirmed empty.

## What actually worked: a seventh family the brief didn't name

Local and trade news coverage of **specific, named, current utility-conflict
delays**, where a beat reporter directly quotes the county/city engineer of
record. This is the same structural move as the brief's core thesis (public
record beats forums) one level down: these aren't survey responses, they're
contemporaneous news quotes tied to a real, ongoing project.

**Important scope caveat, stated up front:** every quote below comes from a
public-agency county/city engineer or public-works director on a general
road/utility-relocation project. The scan's UCM buyer is specifically "an
individual PE/EIT at a *private* transportation or land-development design
firm" working the SHRP2 UCM methodology. None of these speakers are that
buyer — they're the public-agency side of the same conflict, one hop away
from the buyer, not the buyer's own voice. They evidence that **the pain is
real, current, and costs real money** (which the scan already had, from FHWA/
GAO sources); they do not evidence that this specific private-firm buyer
feels it the same way. Flagging this rather than letting scope silently
drift — worth a product call on whether agency-side pain counts for this
copy, or whether direct EIT/PE outreach is still required for gate item 3.

## Discard rate and what it means

8 candidates surfaced, 5 clearly usable (1–5), 2 borderline (6–7), 1 excluded
by design (8). Roughly matches the brief's expected discard rate. Combined
with the top-3 ranked families coming back genuinely empty (not just
hard-to-search), I'd call this: **named pain is no longer "unevidenced" for
the general utility-conflict problem** — it's evidenced, dated, sourced, and
attributable. What remains open, and is a product/copy decision rather than
a research one, is whether public-agency-side quotes satisfy gate item 3 for
a product sold to private-firm PE/EITs, or whether Scribe's copy needs a
quote from that specific buyer role before headline use.

## Recommendation

- Usable now if agency-side pain is acceptable: candidates 1–3 are strong
  enough to headline-test directly (Pfitzer's contractor-quote-within-a-quote
  in particular reads like ad copy already).
- If the private-firm-buyer distinction matters for Anti-BS Rule 11 /
  gate item 3 specifically: this pass doesn't close that gap, and neither
  would more of the same search — it needs direct outreach to a PE/EIT at a
  transportation design firm (the access question joseleos has had open
  since the original scan), not another round of news search.
- NCHRP Synthesis 506 / 583 case-examples chapters remain a real, unchecked
  lead — flagging for whoever has NAP/TRB member access or a paid PDF to look
  at Ch. 3–4 of each before concluding those synthesis reports are empty too.
