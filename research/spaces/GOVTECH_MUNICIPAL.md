# Space profile: municipal govtech (Texas beachhead)

Written for the `financial-model` run on Strata (2026-08-30). It is a space profile, not a
Strata document: `venture-discovery`, `go-to-market`, and `financial-model` read it for buyer
archetype, sales motion, pricing norms, and the liability analog. Sources are the Strata
research and GTM docs (`~/Documents/Strata_Market_Research.docx`, `Strata_GTM_Strategy.docx`,
2026-08-29) plus the statutes cited inline.

**Scope:** software sold to municipal governments (cities, towns, villages) and their elected
or appointed officials; Texas first (1,224 municipalities; ~230 in the 10k–50k tier).

**Buyer archetypes:** city manager (economic buyer; controls the software line); mayor /
council member (champion and user; no staff of their own); city clerk (gatekeeper of the
record — ally or blocker); IT director (security questionnaire, only in 50k+ cities);
appointed board/commission volunteers (secondary users, outnumber officials ~5:1).

**Sales motion:** founder-led demo, advisor warm intros, association events; free or
low-cost pilot in exchange for a named testimonial; 4–8 weeks from demo to signature under
the bid threshold; renewals and new purchases cluster around budget adoption (Texas city
fiscal years mostly begin Oct 1; budgets adopted Aug–Sep, Tex. Loc. Gov't Code ch. 102).

**Pricing norms:** tiered by population, not per seat ("no per-seat fees" is the category
norm — Ordinal AI); small/mid cities $3k–$30k/yr, AI-native tools at the low end; one-time
onboarding fee $500–2k; annual invoice, paid by ACH/check; municipalities are exempt from
Texas sales tax on the purchase (Tex. Tax Code §151.309), so no tax pass-through.

**Procurement facts that drive the model:**
- Competitive bidding required above **$50,000** per contract (Tex. Loc. Gov't Code
  §252.021); purchases $3k–$50k need quotes from two HUB vendors where practicable (§252.0215).
  Below that, the city manager can usually sign. Keep ACV under $50k; many charters set lower
  internal thresholds — verify per city.
- Cooperative purchasing (BuyBoard, TIPS, DIR) is the shortcut for larger cities; TX-RAMP
  (Tex. Gov't Code §2054.0593) binds state agencies, not cities, but cities borrow its
  questionnaire.
- Public Information Act (Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552): contracts and data handling are public
  records; nothing a vendor stores for a city is private from requestors.
- Certificate of insurance (GL + cyber, typically $1M) is a routine pre-condition for data
  access — an early G&A line, not a later one.

**Named incumbents (price against them):** Granicus, CivicPlus, OpenGov, Municode,
NovusAgenda, GovPilot (workflow suites); Ordinal AI, ClerkMinutes, Aware, CivicSummary
(AI-native). The real incumbent is the manual workaround: Ctrl+F through the packet and
calling the clerk.

**Liability analog for the rubric:** does the output become part of the official record or
determine a vote? Strata-class tools *assist* (cite the page; official verifies) — errors are
embarrassment and churn, not statutory liability. Tools that draft minutes or ordinances sit
closer to the record and carry more.

**Where unprompted complaints live:** council-meeting minutes and video (public record —
officials complaining on the record about packet size and late delivery); TML *Texas Town &
City*; ELGL; ICMA forums; r/localgovernment; city-clerk associations (TMCA).

**Channels:** Texas Municipal League (annual conference Nov 11–13 2026 San Antonio; region
meetings; Small City Sessions), TCMA (city managers), ELGL, GovTech.com, LinkedIn public-
sector; advisor-led intros are the highest-ROI channel at zero budget.

**Financial-model hooks:** retention is high once a tool is in the budget (public-sector
SaaS gross retention commonly reported 90–95% — vendor-reported, no independent source yet,
tag L); COGS is LLM inference + retrieval per city and scales with packet volume, not seats;
seasonality — expect Q4 (Oct–Dec) starts and Aug–Sep renewals; the ACV ceiling for a
no-bid sale is the §252.021 threshold.
